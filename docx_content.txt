Recommended direction

Make the website a practical introduction to Pixel’s six services, with Oman as the primary market. Explain what each service includes, show useful demonstrations, and make it easy for visitors to describe their needs. Build a separate, genuinely useful Thrissur page rather than mixing both markets into every headline.

Decisions before development

Replace broad software and enterprise AI positioning with the confirmed website, search, social media and hospitality services. Retain software engineering claims only if Pixel confirms those capabilities.

Use one primary action across the site: Discuss your project. Use Try the sample QR menu as a secondary action on hospitality content.

Remove all dummy testimonials, logos, numbers, staff biographies, prices and contact details. Do not publish Adox projects or history as Pixel work.

Keep the present visual identity if it passes a separate mobile and desktop review. A full redesign is not justified by the available evidence alone.

Proposed navigation

Home | Services | QR Menu Demo | About | Insights | Contact. Under Services, list all six services. Link the Thrissur page from the service-area section and footer, not as a competing homepage headline. Add Work only when permission-cleared projects are available.

Publishing instructions

The copy in this plan is proposed website text. Confirm delivery scope before publishing promises about process or features. Square-bracketed fields are owner inputs and must be replaced or removed. Internal build notes are not website copy. Arabic content should be professionally reviewed before launch; do not imply a local office or language capability that has not been confirmed.

Suggested build order

First: core pages, truthful business details and working contact journey. Next: sample QR menu and needs assessment. Then: useful Oman guides, approved work examples and the Thrissur page. Search visibility is an ongoing effort, not a guaranteed ranking outcome.

Innovative features with a practical purpose

Priority one guided needs assessment

Build a short, optional questionnaire, not a fake website audit. Ask the visitor’s market, business type, website status and main goal. Show the recommendation without requiring contact details. Offer an optional enquiry after the result.

Suggested rules: no website + enquiries goal → website planning; website exists + search goal → SEO review; local customer visits + profile help → eligibility discussion for Google Business Profile; restaurant or hotel + menu goal → QR menu; content consistency goal → social media; AI discovery goal → AI visibility with limitations.

Result example: “A website planning conversation may be your best starting point because you said you do not yet have a website and want customers to enquire. This suggestion uses your answers, not a technical inspection.” Allow visitors to change answers and see why the result changes.

Acceptance: no URL scanning, no invented scores, no automatic sales estimate, no data submission before a clear send action. Track starts and completions only through approved, privacy-conscious measurement.

Priority one try the menu

Build a mobile sample with three categories and a small set of sample dishes. Offer a direct link and QR code. Make text sizing and category navigation easy to use. Add Arabic switching only after accurate translation and right-to-left testing. Use demo labels throughout and no order or payment flow.

Priority two project scope builder

Let visitors select needed pages, languages and features, then generate an editable project brief. Display “This is a draft scope, not a quote.” Do not show prices or delivery dates until Pixel approves a real pricing model and capacity assumptions.

Priority two explain the work with evidence

Publish permission-cleared before-and-after project stories: the original problem, exact work, dates, screenshots and limits of the evidence. If reporting results, state the source, comparison periods and known changes such as seasonality or advertising. Avoid invented leads, revenue or ranking gains. Keep concept work labelled as concept work.

Priority three an honest knowledge assistant

Consider a small assistant only after the core content is reliable. Limit answers to approved service information, provide a human contact route and state that quotes need review. Do not let it invent prices, availability or guarantees. A well-organized FAQ is a lower-maintenance starting point.

Recommended first release

Choose the needs assessment and sample QR menu. Both make the site distinctive by helping customers decide, rather than adding distracting animation. Each should remain useful on mobile and keyboard-accessible, with no compulsory login.

Launch checklist and ongoing search work

Before launch

Owner: confirm legal name, contact channels, operating locations, service scope, permissions and project ownership. Remove every dummy claim and unused template page.

Developer: agree the final domain before publishing. Review existing indexed URLs before any migration; preserve useful URLs or plan appropriate redirects. Do not change domains solely on an untested ranking assumption.

Developer: test mobile navigation, links, contact delivery, error handling, keyboard use and readable contrast. Check layout on common screen sizes and optimize heavy images and scripts.

SEO lead: provide descriptive titles, headings and internal links; verify important text is available to crawlers. Check canonical URLs, sitemap, robots rules, status codes and accidental noindex directives. Keep unfinished pages out of indexing.

Content owner: use original, helpful service copy. Add image descriptions where useful and verify image permissions. Match structured data to visible, accurate information; do not add fake ratings.

Owner and developer: approve the privacy notice, form retention, access controls and any analytics or consent setup before collecting visitor data. Keep secrets out of client-side code.

Launch verification

Submit and inspect the sitemap through authorized Search Console access. Check representative live URLs and confirm contact messages reach the correct business inbox. Record the live URLs and release date.

Review eligible Business Profile details with the owner. Verify all public phone, email, map and social links. A website launch does not authorize profile creation or changes.

Record the initial measurement baseline and test enquiry events without collecting sensitive information in analytics. Separate genuine enquiries from spam and tests.

First month after launch

Weekly: check crawl or indexing issues, broken contact journeys and unexpected content problems. Review early queries and page performance without treating small samples as trends. Correct factual errors promptly. Publish the first useful Oman guide based on actual customer questions.

Ongoing monthly work

Review relevant impressions, clicks, qualified enquiries and key page performance. Improve a small number of important pages, maintain business information and publish useful material where there is a real content gap. Keep a change log and compare meaningful periods. Rankings can change; no plan can keep a fixed position permanently.

Evidence boundaries for review

Review hierarchy, navigation, legibility, contrast, spacing, forms and page weight against the proposed content. Confirm technical findings with tests rather than assuming that a modern appearance means a healthy site.
